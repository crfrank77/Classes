# MAE 243 Electric Power Systems Modeling 

MAE 243 sought to have students formulate and solve canonical constrained optimization problems in electric power systems planning and operation, with applications to current and future low-carbon grids. Techno-economic assessment, implementations in a modern algebraic modeling language, and techniques to enhance computational tractability were covered. 

# Final Project
The final project for this course had students analyze unique grid scenarios and build a complex model utilizing the data. My study examines how cost uncertainties in emerging clean energy technologies shape optimal capacity expansion for a fully decarbonized WECC electricity system in 2045. A deterministic linear programming model evaluates fourteen scenarios combining cost reductions for enhanced geothermal systems, small modular reactors, and battery energy storage systems (BESS) under reference and high electrification demand. System costs decline across all cases as technologies become cheaper, with BESS cost reductions producing the largest changes in both cost and deployment. Geothermal expands only when costs fall by more than 50%, while nuclear capacity remains largely unchanged. High electrification scenarios show higher total costs and substantially greater storage deployment, emphasizing the role of flexible resources in meeting clean energy demand. Overall, results highlight the strong influence of technology learning trajectories on system design and investment priorities for a reliable, carbon-free WECC grid.
